01 · Resolution & Conversion

The HD Document Scanner

Scanning a hand-drawn artwork with a cheap printer vs. a high-end studio scanner. A smooth, organic sound wave — a real voice singing — gets converted differently depending on what's doing the converting. Switch between three converter qualities and hear the difference.

The take-away

A sound card is a high-definition translator — it converts real-world air movement into digital computer data. Higher bit-depth and sample rate (like the 24-bit/96kHz studio interface) mean less of the original performance gets thrown away in that translation.

02 · Latency & Real-Time Monitoring

The Echo-Free Mirror

Ever tried speaking on a video call when your own voice comes back to you half a second later? It makes you stutter. Switch between a standard computer jack and a studio interface to hear how much that round-trip delay changes.

The take-away

A studio audio interface processes sound at ultra-fast speeds — under 3 milliseconds — so artists can hear themselves back in real time without a distracting delay. Anywhere past roughly 10ms, the brain can't reconcile "what I just sang" with "what I'm hearing," and performers start to stumble.
