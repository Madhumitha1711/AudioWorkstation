Same source, different rooms

We recorded the same sound in five different places. Listen to each one. The sound didn't change, only the room did.

Kept the same every time: source, microphone, mic distance, recording level, no effects.

Why this matters

  • The mic records the room too. It can't separate the source from the echoes, so both end up in the recording.
  • You can add echo later, but you can't remove it. A dry recording can be given any reverb in the mix. A recording made in a bathroom stays a bathroom recording.
  • Echo affects clarity. Too much of it blurs words and notes together and pushes the sound further back.
  • Takes need to match. Recording in a controlled room means a retake tomorrow will sound the same as today's.
Takeaway: before reaching for EQ or plugins, get the room right. The room shapes the sound before anything else does.
