    Why choose one mic over another?

    Every recommendation above comes from the same six questions. Learn these and you can reason your way to a good choice with any mic locker, even one you’ve never seen before.

    01 · Loudness

    Can it take the level?

    A kick drum or cranked amp can hit 130 dB+ at the grille. Dynamics barely notice; sensitive condensers may need a pad, and a ribbon can be damaged by a blast of air.

    02 · Detail

    How much detail do you need?

    A lighter diaphragm follows air pressure faster. That’s why condensers win on breath, finger noise, cymbal shimmer and acoustic transients, and dynamics sound a little rounder.

    03 · Room

    Do you want the room in it?

    A sensitive mic hears the room as clearly as the source. In an untreated room or on stage, a less sensitive, tight-pattern mic gives a cleaner take. In a treated studio, that sensitivity becomes an asset.

    04 · Pattern

    Where should it not listen?

    Cardioid rejects the back (monitors, other players). Figure-8 rejects the sides and hears front and back. Omni hears everything, sounds most natural and has no proximity effect.

    05 · Character

    What colour do you want?

    Neutral and detailed (FET condenser), warm and flattering (tube), smooth and dark (ribbon), punchy midrange (dynamic). Pick the mic whose colour moves the source toward the sound you want.

    06 · Practical

    Will it work on the day?

    Power (48 V phantom, tube PSU), fragility, size, handling noise and cost. A budget dynamic that survives a stage beats an expensive tube mic that can’t go on one.

    Takeaway: start from the source and the room, not the price tag. Choose the mic that solves your biggest problem first (level, spill or harshness), then choose for character.
