Train your ears before you train your hands.

Three quick experiments — no theory yet, just listening. Put in headphones or turn your speakers up, then tap play on any experiment below. Real recordings, nothing processed live.

01 · Speaker Test

Car Stereo vs. Kitchen Radio

Why does your favorite song sound amazing in your car, but thin and terrible off a phone speaker on the kitchen counter? Same song, three real recordings of it — captured through each system.

The take-away

Consumer speakers lie to you on purpose — they boost bass or treble to sound exciting in a showroom. Studio monitors tell the flat, boring truth, so if a mix sounds right on them, it'll translate everywhere else too.

02 · Room Acoustics

The Singing-in-the-Shower Effect

Why does everyone sound like a pop star singing in a tiled bathroom? Drag the speaker between three real rooms and hear the same note recorded in each one — nothing about the source changed, only the room around it.

The take-away

What you're hearing isn't just the sound — it's the room bouncing that sound back at you. Tile reflects almost everything; coats absorb almost everything. Studio monitors need a treated room so the space stops adding its own opinion to your mix.

03 · Stereo Imaging

The Headphone Illusion

Put on headphones and a sound can feel like it's sitting inside your skull. Play the same phrase on speakers and it feels like a stage in front of you. Toggle between a speaker recording and a headphone recording of the same moment and hear the difference.

Audio: audio/module3-speakers.mp3 · audio/module3-headphones.mp3 (same phrase, recorded via speakers vs. headphones)
The take-away

Real speakers mix sound in open air before it reaches both your ears — each ear hears a little of both speakers. Headphones skip that step entirely, feeding each ear in total isolation. That's the "3D stage" speakers create naturally, and what stereo mixing has to fake for headphones.
